# Resound product brief

## Product

Resound helps music students retain and act on what happened in a lesson. A student records a lesson moment, a teacher's explanation, or a quick reflection afterward. Resound keeps the original media, turns the useful parts into clips and notes, and makes them easy to find when practice time arrives.

## People

The primary user is a musician taking recurring lessons or coaching. They may be a beginner building a habit, a serious student working toward an audition, or a working musician refining a particular skill. Teachers and coaches are a future second user type. Their value is in shaping the student's next actions, not in managing a generic classroom.

## Problem

Lessons produce too much information to remember. Notes are incomplete, recordings are long and hard to revisit, and the advice that matters most gets lost before the next practice session.

## Product promise

After a lesson, a student can answer three questions in under a minute:

1. What did my teacher want me to work on?
2. What did it sound or look like when they explained it?
3. What should I practice next?

## Core loop

Capture a lesson moment. Save or edit the important excerpt. Attach a short takeaway and a theme. Return during practice, replay it, and mark the work as revisited or complete.

## Product principles

- Preserve the source. AI suggestions should always lead back to the original moment.
- Make review more valuable than storage. A growing library is only useful if it changes what the student practices.
- Keep capture fast enough for a lesson. Rich organization can happen afterward.
- Treat a teacher's advice as personal context. Sharing should be deliberate and legible.

## First success signal

A student records a lesson, saves at least one takeaway, then opens and replays it before their next lesson.
